# VVS-0001 — Voryn Versioning Specification

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **State** | ACCEPTED |
| **Date** | 2026-10-22 |
| **Scope** | The whole VorynOS project |

## 1. Objective

Define a single, predictable and auditable versioning policy for the
VorynOS and for the contracts and formats that require stable compatibility.

## 2. Format

```text
MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH[-CHANNEL.N][+BUILD]
```

- `MAJOR`: incompatible change of contract, format or architecture.
- `MINOR`: new feature that is compatible with the contract in force.
- `PATCH`: correction of a defect without a change of contract.
- `CHANNEL.N`: channel and count. Valid channels, in order of increasing
  stability: `dev`, `alpha`, `beta`, `rc`.
- `BUILD`: build metadata, free suffix after `+`, for automated tooling (for
  example `+20261021.3`). It does not change the order of precedence.

## 3. Channels

| Channel | Use | Stability |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| `dev` | Work in progress, without contract guarantee | None |
| `alpha` | Incomplete feature, unstable contract | Low |
| `beta` | Complete feature, frozen contract | Medium |
| `rc` | Release candidate | High |
| (no channel) | Release | Maximum |

Rules:

1. The removal of the channel suffix is a conscious action, and not an
   automatic one.
2. `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` without a channel means a stable version.
3. The `CHANNEL.N` part restarts on every increment of `MAJOR.MINOR`.
4. Without a channel, a correction is a `PATCH`; with a channel, it is an
   increment of `N`.

## 4. Contract compatibility

Contracts that require stable compatibility are versioned
**independently** of the version of the system:

| Contract | Own version | Where it lives |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| VNB → VNK handoff contract | `handoff_version` in `shared/handoff.zig` | `docs/specs/boot-handoff-spec.md` |
| VEX format | `VEX` | `docs/specs/` (planned) |
| VPK format | `VPK` | `docs/specs/` (planned) |
| Syscall ABI | `syscall_abi` | `docs/specs/syscall-abi.md` |

Compatibility rules:

1. A field is never removed from a versioned contract; it is reserved.
2. New fields are added at the end of the structure, and the `header_size`
   or the entry size is incremented.
3. The consumer **has to** reject an unknown version, instead of
   interpreting it by guesswork. The handoff contract already does that:
   the magic and the version are verified before any use.
4. A change of layout of a contract requires a `MAJOR` of the system or a
   bigger version of the contract itself, with proof that both sides agree.

## 5. States and version

- A feature that does not exist yet **does not** get a release version.
- The version of the system describes the **state of the code**, and not
  the intention.
- The version number of a milestone (F0, F1) is registered in
  `docs/roadmap/phase-status.md` together with the real state.

## 6. Native formats

Each native format (`.vex`, `.vlb`, `.vpk`, `.vdrv`, `.vcfg`, `.varc`,
`.vimg`) carries, in its header:

- `magic` of 8 bytes;
- `format_version` of 16 bits, independent of the version of the system;
- `header_size` of 16 bits;
- `flags` of 32 bits, with the highest bit reserved for "mandatory
  extension" (a reader that does not know the extension has to refuse the
  file).

## 7. Prohibitions

1. Do not publish a tag or a release without explicit authorization.
2. Do not reuse a version number that was already published.
3. Do not assign a `MAJOR` greater than necessary: the major version is
   reserved for a real break of contract.
4. Do not declare a feature as "stable" while it is not exercised by the
   corresponding automated test.

## 8. Extraordinary exception of 2026-10-22

Rule 2 of section 7 **was not changed**. It remains in force, and it holds
for the future. What exists here is a **single and extraordinary
exception**, registered for the reorganization of the public versioning
milestones described in
[`../releases/VERSIONING-RESET-2026.md`](../releases/VERSIONING-RESET-2026.md).

### 8.1 What the exception authorizes

The exception authorizes the publication of `v0.1.0-dev.1` as the new
official initial milestone, and the discontinuation of the identifiers
`v0.1.0-dev.2` and `v0.1.0-dev.3`, with the future reuse of those last two
identifiers for **new** releases.

The exception also authorizes the **republication** of `v0.1.0-dev.1` over
a later commit, with the removal of the previous release and tag. The
republication is the same operation, with the same effect: the identifier
stops pointing to the old experimental milestone, and starts pointing to the
consolidated and validated state. The procedure is in
[`../releases/VERSIONING-RESET-2026.md`](../releases/VERSIONING-RESET-2026.md).

### 8.2 Why the exception is necessary

1. **The old versions were experimental milestones.** They were published
   in the `dev` channel, which has no contract guarantee, and each one
   covered only a slice of the boot path. They did not constitute a
   baseline, and the numbering of the channel came to suggest an order of
   progress that did not correspond to the real state of the code.
2. **The old identifiers were discontinued.** The tags and the releases
   `v0.1.0-dev.2` and `v0.1.0-dev.3` were removed, and the procedure is
   registered in the cited document.
3. **The source code was not reverted.** No line of the published code was
   erased, and no feature was discarded. The new version carries all the
   progress that existed.
4. **The original commits remain preserved.** The commits
   `42c3e62fbf84798d07cf36c3461d6b73e51cbb0f` and
   `5dd258830823ca8a9d9a257460643c7924ee23d9` were neither rewritten nor
   erased: they remain reachable in the archive branch
   `archive/versioning-reset-2026`, and the technical state that they
   recorded is transcribed in the historical documentation. The archive
   branch does not replace the history of `main`, and exists only to
   preserve the reference of the discontinued milestones after the removal
   of the tags that pointed to them.
5. **The new sequence begins at `v0.1.0-dev.1`**, and it is the consolidated
   initial milestone of the project.
6. **The future `dev.2` and `dev.3` will be new releases**, and each one
   of them will correspond to a verifiable technical increment.
7. **The future releases will point to different commits from the old
   milestones.** No reused identifier points again to
   `42c3e62fbf84798d07cf36c3461d6b73e51cbb0f` or to
   `5dd258830823ca8a9d9a257460643c7924ee23d9`.
8. **The reuse of the names can cause ambiguities** in clones, in network
   caches and in external systems that hold the old reference under the same
   identifier. This is why the discontinuation is registered in a document
   of its own, and is not done in silence.
9. **The exception does not establish a general permission to reuse
   published versions.** It holds only for the two identifiers
   `v0.1.0-dev.2` and `v0.1.0-dev.3`, and for the republication of
   `v0.1.0-dev.1`, on this date, and only for the reorganization
   described. Any future reuse of a published identifier is prohibited, as
   it always was.

### 8.3 What the exception does not do

- It does not rewrite the commit history of `main`.
- It does not erase the branch `main`, nor tags, nor releases, beyond the
  two identifiers that are expressly authorized.
- It does not change the rules of format, of channel, of contract
  compatibility, nor the other prohibitions of this specification.
- It does not declare any feature stable, and it does not change the
  technical state of any milestone.
